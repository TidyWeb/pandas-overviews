Explore the dataset before the analysis.

Topic 7 provides a practical first pass: inspect structure, preview rows, understand categories and frequencies, summarise distributions, and examine numerical relationships.

Exploration order: structure first, then sample rows, types and labels, categories and frequencies, summary statistics, and finally correlation.

03 / CATEGORIES

Understand what repeats, then count how often.

Use unique values to see the categories and frequency counts to see their distribution.

Distinct valuesList and count categories
df['Col3'].unique()
df['Col3'].nunique()

For Col3: the distinct values are A, B, C, and D; the unique count is 4.

FrequenciesCount absolute or relative levels
df['Col3'].value_counts()
df['Col3'].value_counts(
    normalize=True
)

Counts: A=5, B=2, C=2, D=1. Relative proportions are 0.5, 0.2, 0.2, and 0.1.

Practice transfer: apply unique(), nunique(), and both forms of value_counts() to df_practice['region'] and df_practice['children'].

04 / SUMMARY

Summarise distributions and relationships.

.describe() summarises columns; .corr() compares numerical variables.

StatisticsNumerical or all columns
df.describe()
df.describe(include='all')

Result: counts, means, spread, quartiles, and extremes; include='all' also considers categorical columns.

CorrelationNumerical relationships
df.corr(
    numeric_only=True
)
df.corr(
    method='spearman',
    numeric_only=True
)

Result: a correlation matrix. Pearson is linear; Spearman is monotonic.

Current Pandas compatibility: the lesson’s plain df.corr() includes text-like Col3 and raises a conversion error in Pandas 3.0.1. Restrict the calculation to numeric columns with numeric_only=True.
